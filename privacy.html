Sorta is a software layer that helps independent medical and dental clinics automate their paperwork: a patient fills out their information once through a link, and that information is distributed across the forms the clinic already uses (intake, consent, HIPAA-style acknowledgement, insurance, clinical history), producing a locked PDF ready to review and sign. The link reaches the patient by email and by WhatsApp, where an automated assistant also offers a simple menu (schedule, reschedule, talk to the clinic) and routes the conversation to a staff member when needed.

This notice serves three purposes at once, and its structure reflects all three:

  • It is Sorta's own privacy policy as a company — for clinic staff who use the platform and for visitors to this site.
  • It is the document Meta / the WhatsApp Business Platform requires to be publicly posted and current for a Tech Provider operating on the WhatsApp Business API.
  • It is a reference clinics can cite when drafting their own patient-facing privacy notice, since it accurately describes how Sorta processes the data a clinic entrusts to it. No clinic is exempt from maintaining its own notice — see section 18.

2. Key definitions

  • Personal data: any information that identifies or could identify a person (name, phone number, email, address).
  • Sensitive personal data: data whose misuse could cause greater harm — in particular, health data (clinical history, diagnoses, treatment). We handle this category with heightened care throughout this notice.
  • Controller: whoever decides why and how data is used, and holds the direct relationship with the person the data is about.
  • Processor: whoever handles personal data on a controller's behalf and instructions, without independently deciding why it's used.
  • Data subject: the person the personal data is about (the patient, the staff member, the site visitor).

3. Who this applies to — and Sorta's role

Sorta plays a different role depending on whose data is involved. This distinction matters throughout the document:

  • Patient data: Sorta acts as a processor. The clinic is the controller — it decides what data is requested, how it's used clinically, and holds the direct relationship with the patient. Sorta processes that data only per the clinic's instructions, expressed through its form configuration.
  • Clinic staff data (accounts): Sorta acts as the controller. We decide how this data is used (running the account, support, billing) within what's described here.
  • Website visitors: Sorta acts as the controller.

4. Information we collect

4.1 Patient information (collected on the clinic's behalf)

  • Full name, date of birth, phone number, email, and home address.
  • Insurance details.
  • Health and clinical information — reason for visit, history, symptoms, and whatever else a clinic's form requests. This is sensitive personal data.
  • Consent acknowledgements and electronic signatures captured within forms.
  • Every answer entered into a clinic's forms.
  • Immutable snapshots of each form at the moment it's saved, and the final generated PDF — creating a dated record of what was completed at each visit.
  • WhatsApp-specific data: the patient's WhatsApp phone number, conversation state with the automated assistant (e.g., which menu option was chosen), and — only for conversations escalated to a human — the message text, retained for [CONFIRM: 90] days and then purged. On first contact, patients receive a one-time notice advising them not to send detailed clinical information over that channel.

4.2 Clinic staff information (accounts)

  • Email address and password — stored hashed with bcrypt; we never store or can read it in plain text.
  • Role and the workspace (clinic account) they belong to.

4.3 Audit log

Every meaningful action inside the platform (who did what, to which record, and when) is written to an immutable audit log — protecting both the clinic and the patient with a verifiable trail.

4.4 Form access links

Links that let a patient fill out or view their forms require no login: they're a unique token that automatically expires 7 days after it's generated.

5. Why we use it

  • Patient data: used exclusively to prepare, sync, and store the paperwork for a visit per the clinic's instructions. Never used for marketing. Never sold. Legal basis: consent given to the clinic (section 6) and the contractual relationship between Sorta and the clinic to provide the service.
  • Clinic staff data: to run the account, provide support, bill, and communicate relevant service changes. Legal basis: the Sorta–clinic contractual relationship.
  • Website visitor data: [CONFIRM] — typically, understanding site usage and improving content.

6. How consent is obtained

Because most of this data is health data (sensitive personal data), meaningful, informed consent is required. The clinic collects it — that's the clinic's responsibility as data controller, typically when sharing the intake link or during form completion, per the clinic's own privacy notice. Sorta provides the infrastructure to capture and evidence that consent (in-form confirmations, the immutable snapshots described in 4.1), but does not replace a clinic's own notice to its patients.

7. Use of WhatsApp

Sorta uses Meta's WhatsApp Business API for two purposes: (1) sending the patient their form-completion link, and (2) operating an automated assistant offering a simple menu (schedule, reschedule, talk to the clinic) that routes the conversation to a clinic staff member when requested or needed.

  • On first WhatsApp contact, patients receive a notice advising them to avoid sending detailed clinical information over that channel.
  • While a conversation is handled by the automated assistant, Sorta retains the conversation state (which menu option was selected), not free-text content the patient might type.
  • Once a conversation is escalated to a clinic staff member, that message text is retained — for [CONFIRM: 90] days — so the clinic can follow up, then purged.
  • Meta processes message content on its own servers to deliver it; see section 9 on international transfers.
