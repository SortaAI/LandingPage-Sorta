What optometry patient intake typically includes

Optometry intake is uniquely complex because most visits involve two completely different insurance authorizations on the same patient. The routine portion of the visit — annual exam, refraction, contact lens fit, frame allowance — bills through a vision-specific benefit plan like VSP, EyeMed, Davis Vision, or Spectera. The medical portion — dry-eye treatment, glaucoma management, diabetic retinopathy screening, retinal evaluation, foreign body removal — bills through medical insurance with its own authorization and ICD-10 documentation. The same patient often touches both during the same visit, and the staff has to know which form gets filled when.

On top of that, every optometry visit produces a comprehensive eye exam intake (refractive history, current symptoms, family ocular history, medications), a contact lens fitting consent for CL evaluations, a dilation authorization, and a release of information when a finding warrants a referral back to the primary care provider — diabetic retinopathy, hypertensive retinopathy, or a flagged neurologic sign. Without automation, all of those forms inherit the same demographic block that gets typed onto every one separately.

Sorta captures both insurance types and the full refractive and ocular history at intake on the patient's phone before the visit. The right authorization form populates automatically per service. Pre-test techs walk into the room with the chart already filled. The clinical decisions belong to the OD or MD; the paperwork repetition goes away.
